# Research tasks

Six short tasks, **5% each** (30% in total). Each one takes a week's idea out of the notebook and into a real system you know. Write about half a page in your own words, with at least one named source.

| Task | Topic | Set in | Share in the class warm-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A real information system | Week 1 | Week 2 |
| R2 | Rules and edge cases | Week 2 | Week 3 |
| R3 | Checking input | Week 4 (after the midterm) | Week 5 |
| R4 | How systems keep data and history | Week 5 | Week 6 |
| R5 | Keeping data safe | Week 6 | Week 7 |
| R6 | Checking AI output | Week 7 | Week 8 |

There is no research task in the midterm week or in Weeks 9–10, so you can focus on the final project.

**Warm-up:** the first 10 minutes of Session 1. Bring one finding to share in a group of three. **Submit** by email to [submit.progmis@gmail.com](mailto:submit.progmis@gmail.com); deadlines and file names are announced in class. Marking criteria are in [assessment](ASSESSMENT.md).

## R1 · A real information system

Choose a system you have used: school registration, a bus or ticket app, a library, a hospital queue, a mobile-money app. Who uses it (at least two roles)? What data does it keep? Name one rule it follows, and one thing that could go wrong and who would be hurt.

## R2 · Rules and edge cases

Choose a rule from real life: a bus fare for children, a library late fee, a data bundle, an exam pass mark. Write it exactly, the way a program would need it. Give a normal case, a boundary case (exactly at the limit) and an odd case (missing, negative or unusual). Who decides the rule, and who is affected when the boundary is wrong?

## R3 · Checking input

Why do systems check input before saving it? Choose a form you have used. For two of its fields, give the allowed values, an invalid example, the message shown (or the message that should be shown), and what must stay unchanged when the input is bad.

## R4 · How systems keep data and history

Choose a real example: a bank statement, school records, a phone's call log. What is kept? Which IDs are used? When something is corrected, is the old value kept? Who needs the history, and why?

## R5 · Keeping data safe

Learn about backups, audit logs and transactions. Example: a bank transfer has two updates (take money from one account, add it to another) that must succeed or fail together. What could go wrong between two updates? How would you check the records before and after?

## R6 · Checking AI output

Take an AI answer to a factual or technical question (or use the example in the Week 7 notebook). Check it against an original source. List what you would accept, what you would correct and what you would ask about, and say how you checked each one.

## Using AI tools for research

AI tools are allowed. If you use one, show one place where you checked or corrected it, and say how. Your submission must be your own explanation. You can earn full marks without using AI.
