# Assessment

The course assesses what you **understand, build, test and explain**. The weights below are the **proposed plan**; the instructor will confirm them with the university before assessment begins. Calendar deadlines and file names are announced in class.

| Component | Weight | What counts |
|---|---:|---|
| Participation | 10% | Taking part in class activities and the weekly reflection form |
| Six research tasks | 30% | 5% each: what you read or found, a concrete example, a check, and your own reasoning |
| Individual midterm (Week 4) | 30% | Reading, writing and testing code; judging AI code; explaining a decision |
| Final project (Weeks 5–10) | 30% | Your working system, your decisions with evidence, one extension with its tests, a short report, and Demo day |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

**Submit** by email to [submit.progmis@gmail.com](mailto:submit.progmis@gmail.com), unless the instructor announces another route. Keep your own copy of everything you submit.

Read this page at the start of the course, and again before the midterm and the final project. The sections below follow the order of the course itself: taking part each week, the research tasks, the midterm, and then the final project.

## Participation (10%)

Join the Session 1–3 activities: predictions, pair work, Good-or-risky discussions and class warm-ups. After each week, fill in the [weekly reflection form](COURSE_LINKS.md). A missing form is followed up; it is not an automatic absence.

## Research tasks (30%)

Six short tasks, 5% each. R1 and R2 connect a week's idea to a real system. R3–R6 each teach you one programming concept in five steps: read and organise, ask an AI about what stayed unclear, compare and check, your view, sources and honesty. See [research tasks](RESEARCH.md) for the topics, the steps and the schedule.

**R1 and R2**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| A relevant example and at least one named source | 1 |
| An accurate explanation in your own words | 1 |
| A concrete case (for example, an edge case or a real field) | 1 |
| Your reasoning, including one limit or open question | 1 |
| Clear evidence, including how you checked any AI answer | 1 |
| **Total** | **5** |

**R3–R6**

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| **Read and organise:** a summary in your own words (5–8 sentences: what it is, why it exists, how it works, one common mistake) and a tiny example you ran, with its output | 1 |
| **Ask an AI:** 6–10 key lines of the conversation, including your two follow-up questions (an everyday example from Cambodia, and "What can go wrong?"). No AI access: the same with a classmate or the instructor, or your questions answered from a second book chapter | 1 |
| **Compare and check:** one place where the book and the AI agree in different words, differ, or where the AI was vague or wrong; one claim tested by running a tiny example | 1 |
| **Your view:** a clear side on the debate question in 150–250 words, with a reason from the course's case | 1 |
| **Sources and honesty:** the sections and chapters you read and the AI tool you used are named; invented examples only, no real personal data | 1 |
| **Total** | **5** |

For R1 and R2, AI tools are allowed but not needed. If you use one, check at least one claim yourself and say how you checked. Students who do not use AI can earn the same full marks. For R3–R6, you read first; the AI conversation comes after the reading and is part of the task, and comparing and checking it is part of the mark. Your summary and your view must be your own words.

## Midterm (30%)

After the weekly participation and research tasks, the midterm is the first component you complete entirely on your own.

**When:** Week 4, Session 3 (**60 minutes** individual exam + **30 minutes** setup, submission & live debrief). **Access:** [Midterm page](MIDTERM.md) (PIN code shared in class). **Individual work.** Allowed: your own notes, the weekly notebooks and the Python Basics notebook. **Not allowed:** AI tools, messaging or sharing code.

The midterm tests **understanding** of Weeks 1–4 in a new everyday setting (**Campus Library Desk**), not memory.

| Part | Task | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Read & trace code by hand (types, loop trace, `print` vs `return`, records) | 24 |
| 2 | Turn a flowchart into a rule function (`loan_action`) + explain a policy change | 24 |
| 3 | Work with records: `desk_summary(loans)` returns a summary dictionary | 28 |
| 4 | Audit, test & repair AI-written code (`most_overdue_id`) + harm report | 24 |
| End | **My key line** (mandatory ownership check) + clean run | required |

After you submit, a short **2–3 minute oral check**: the instructor asks about your **My key line** and one other line or test from your notebook. If you cannot explain your own code, the marks for that part can be reduced.

## Final project (30%)

The last component brings the whole course together: your own `my_foodbridge/` folder, a working system with your own decisions and one extension of your own, explained in a short report and presented at Demo day. It starts in Week 5 with a three-line project journal at the end of each week, continues with choosing your extension (Week 8) and a project charter (Week 9), and ends with the code freeze and Demo day in Week 10. See the [final project guide](FINAL_PROJECT.md) for the five parts, the timeline, the extension menu and what to submit.

| Part | What is assessed | Points (of 100) |
|---|---|---:|
| **1 · It runs** | your parts pass `bridge.check("all")`, your menu works, your zip is complete | 25 |
| **2 · Make it yours** | three settings you changed (one about privacy or input checks, one backed by data), each with a decision-log row with evidence | 20 |
| **3 · My extension** | one extension from the menu that works, with at least two tests in `tests.py` that can fail | 20 |
| **4 · The report** | `README.md`, about two pages: a drawing of your system, what each user sees, your decision log, your extension, your verification log, how you used AI, two known limits and a reflection | 15 |
| **5 · Demo day (Week 10)** | 3 minutes with your system poster, a live change asked by the instructor, and one question | 20 |

All five parts are required, and these points are proposed (see above). A small system with strong, well-explained decisions and one well-tested extension scores higher than a big one with weak reasons.

## Fairness and integrity

The same fairness rules apply to every component above.

- Your submitted work must be your own, and you must be able to explain it.
- Share ideas and help classmates understand; do not share complete answers to assessed tasks.
- If illness or an emergency affects a deadline, email the instructor as early as you can.
