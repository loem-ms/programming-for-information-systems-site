# Assessment

The course assesses what you **understand, build, test and explain**. The weights below are the **proposed plan**; the instructor will confirm them with the university before assessment begins. Calendar deadlines and file names are announced in class.

| Component | Weight | What counts |
|---|---:|---|
| Participation | 10% | Taking part in class activities and the weekly reflection form |
| Six research tasks | 30% | 5% each: sources, a concrete example and your own reasoning |
| Individual midterm (Week 4) | 30% | Reading, writing and testing code; judging AI code; explaining a decision |
| Final project (Weeks 9–10) | 30% | Your working system, your decisions, verification, handoff and presentation |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

**Submit** by email to [submit.progmis@gmail.com](mailto:submit.progmis@gmail.com), unless the instructor announces another route. Keep your own copy of everything you submit.

## Participation (10%)

Join the Session 1–3 activities: predictions, pair work, Good-or-risky discussions and class warm-ups. After each week, fill in the [weekly reflection form](COURSE_LINKS.md). A missing form is followed up; it is not an automatic absence.

## Research tasks (30%)

Six short tasks, 5% each. Each one connects a week's idea to a real system. See [research tasks](RESEARCH.md) for the topics and schedule.

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| A relevant example and at least one named source | 1 |
| An accurate explanation in your own words | 1 |
| A concrete case (for example, a boundary case or a real field) | 1 |
| Your reasoning, including one limit or open question | 1 |
| Clear evidence, including how you checked any AI answer | 1 |
| **Total** | **5** |

AI tools are allowed for research. If you use one, check at least one claim yourself and say how you checked. Students who do not use AI can earn the same full marks.

## Midterm (30%)

**When:** Week 4, Session 3 (90 minutes). **Notebook:** `Midterm.ipynb`. **Individual work.** Allowed: your own notes, the weekly notebooks and the Python Basics notebook. **Not allowed:** AI tools, messaging or sharing code.

The midterm tests **understanding** of Weeks 1–4, not memory.

| Part | Task | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Read five short pieces of code and explain *why* they behave as they do | 20 |
| B | Write a rule: `volunteers_needed(portions)` with a named constant | 20 |
| C | Work with records: `plan_day(offers)` returns a summary of the day | 25 |
| D | Write tests that catch planted bugs | 15 |
| E | Judge AI-written code: find the problems and who they harm | 10 |
| F | Make a decision, rerun everything cleanly, and log it | 10 |

After you submit, a **3-minute oral check**: the instructor points at one line of your code and one test, and you explain them. If you cannot explain your own code, the marks for that part can be reduced.

## Final project (30%)

See the [final project guide](FINAL_PROJECT.md) for the four levels and what to submit.

| What is assessed | Weight within the project |
|---|---:|
| **It runs and is verified:** Level 1, clean run, `verify()` healthy | 25% |
| **Your decisions:** Level 2 settings and the quality of the decision log | 30% |
| **Tests and extension:** tests for your own decisions (expected) + Level 3 / Level 4 (optional) | 15% |
| **Handoff:** handoff note and peer test record | 15% |
| **Presentation and individual explanation** (Week 10) | 15% |

Completing Levels 1 and 2 well, with two tests for your own decisions, a clear handoff and a presentation, is enough for a good grade. Levels 3 and 4 add marks, but a long program with weak decisions scores lower than a short one with strong, well-explained decisions.

## Fairness and integrity

- Your submitted work must be your own, and you must be able to explain it.
- Share ideas and help classmates understand; do not share complete answers to assessed tasks.
- If illness or an emergency affects a deadline, email the instructor as early as you can.
